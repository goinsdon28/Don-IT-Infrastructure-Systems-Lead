Fourteen Weeks

This journal began as a simple experiment.

I wanted to document what happened when someone with years of systems, networking, infrastructure, and troubleshooting experience stepped into a large-scale data-center environment and started again at the technician level.

The early questions were technical:

  • Could I learn the environment?
  • Could I understand the architecture?
  • Could I diagnose the failures?
  • Could I close the tickets?

Over time, those questions changed.

  • Could I identify incorrect diagnostic paths?
  • Could I prevent unnecessary work?
  • Could I explain the reasoning to another technician?
  • Could I develop technicians who no longer needed me beside them?
  • Could my contribution become larger than my own ticket queue?

The Journal Changed With the Work

The progression moved through several stages:

Learning

Understanding the tools, infrastructure, ticket workflow, physical environment, terminology, and expectations.

Diagnosing

Moving from following steps to understanding evidence and determining what the problem actually was.

Resolving

Taking independent ownership of increasingly complex work and carrying tickets through to defensible conclusions.

Mentoring

Helping other technicians understand the same systems and reasoning.

Teaching

Developing a repeatable way to transfer technical reasoning to an entire cohort.

Developing Others

Measuring success not only by the problems I solved, but by the people who could solve problems independently after working with me.

The Question Underneath the Journal

There was always another question underneath all of this.

Could the effort eventually become something larger?

Could the technical progression, mentoring, training, documentation, and operational impact lead to greater responsibility?

Could it become a formal advancement?

Could it become a different role entirely?

Could another opportunity change the direction?

What Happened Next?

The first fourteen weeks document the preparation.

What comes next documents the outcome.

Did I get the job?

Did the opportunity become something larger?

Did the training work develop into something permanent?

Did another opportunity change the direction entirely?


Was all of this worth it?

Check Back at the Six-Month Mark

At the time this page was written, I did not know the answer.

Several paths were still possible.

None were guaranteed.

That is intentional.

This journal was written while the journey was happening — not rewritten afterward to make the path appear obvious.

The next entry will not rewrite the prediction. It will record what actually happened.
