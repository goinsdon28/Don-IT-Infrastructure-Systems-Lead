14 Weeks of Progression

This journal documents my progression from entering an unfamiliar large-scale data-center environment to independent diagnostics, complex ticket ownership, mentoring, formal technician instruction, and technical leadership.

It was written while the progression was actually happening.

The journal was not created afterward to make the path look cleaner, faster, or more predictable than it really was.

The progression can be summarized as:

Observe → Learn → Diagnose → Resolve → Validate → Teach → Improve → Lead

The public version is intentionally sanitized. Internal systems, identifying infrastructure information, customer-sensitive data, and proprietary processes have been removed or generalized.

Phase I — Foundation

The first three weeks were about understanding the environment, learning the tools, developing safe habits, and moving from observation toward independent ownership.

Week 1 — Entering the Data Center

The first week focused on understanding an unfamiliar large-scale data-center environment: hardware, terminology, workflow, ticketing, fiber, tooling, and operational expectations.

The goal was simple: observe carefully, learn quickly, and avoid creating additional problems.

Week 2 — Building the Foundation

The second week moved from basic observation toward understanding how the environment fit together.

Tool use, fiber handling, ticket context, CLI interaction, and evidence collection began forming a repeatable troubleshooting foundation.

Read Week 2 — Building the Foundation →

Week 3 — From Observation to Ownership

By Week 3, the objective was no longer simply watching other technicians.

Independent ticket ownership began replacing observation as diagnostic confidence increased.

The focus shifted toward forming hypotheses, gathering evidence, taking justified action, and documenting the result.

Read Week 3 — From Observation to Ownership →

Phase II — Independence & Team Effect

Weeks 4 through 7 marked the transition from becoming independently productive to beginning to influence the effectiveness of technicians working around me.

Week 4 — Teaching What I Just Learned

Technical learning began turning outward.

As I became more comfortable with the environment, technicians began shadowing my work and asking questions about the CLI, troubleshooting, and ticket workflow.

Teaching became another way of testing whether I actually understood what I had learned.

Read Week 4 — Teaching What I Just Learned →

Week 5 — Resolution Over Activity

The focus shifted away from simply touching tickets and toward producing defensible technical resolutions.

The objective became: understand the problem, perform the correct work, and leave the ticket better than I found it.

Read Week 5 — Resolution Over Activity →

Week 6 — Scale and Team Effect

Higher ticket volume created a different challenge: maintaining diagnostic quality while increasing operational output.

The week also reinforced that individual productivity can scale when knowledge is shared instead of protected.

Read Week 6 — Scale and Team Effect →

Week 7 — Diagnostic Depth

The troubleshooting became more complex.

Power conditions, management-state anomalies, fiber paths, missing components, and contradictory system evidence required more careful separation of symptoms from root causes.

Read Week 7 — Diagnostic Depth →

Phase III — Advanced Diagnostics

Weeks 8 through 10 moved beyond basic ticket execution into deeper technical judgment, evidence-driven troubleshooting, and systems-level thinking.

Week 8 — Judgment and Advancement

Technical maturity increasingly meant knowing not only what could be done, but what should be done next.

Ticket prioritization, SLA awareness, topology verification, and diagnostic judgment became increasingly important.

Read Week 8 — Judgment and Advancement →

Week 9 — Mentorship and Advanced Diagnostics

Mentoring became more deliberate while diagnostic work continued to deepen.

Technicians were guided through health checks, power conditions, ticket documentation, escalation decisions, and the reasoning required to distinguish a real hardware failure from a misleading symptom.

Read Week 9 — Mentorship and Advanced Diagnostics →

Week 10 — Systems-Level Troubleshooting

The work increasingly required understanding relationships between hardware, provisioning, firmware, interfaces, inventory systems, and operational state.

The question was becoming less: “Which part should I replace?”

And more: “What evidence proves where the failure actually exists?”

Read Week 10 — Systems-Level Troubleshooting →

Phase IV — Technical Leadership

Weeks 11 through 14 document the point where the work began shifting from individual technical execution toward multiplying the capability of other technicians.

Week 11 — Watch Don

Mentoring and technical support became increasingly visible.

Multiple technicians were being coached through live troubleshooting, while increasingly complex investigations continued in parallel.

The week became evidence that technical influence did not require formal authority.

Read Week 11 — Watch Don →

Week 12 — From Technician to Technical Leader

The transition from informal mentoring toward deliberate technician development became clearer.

Technicians were no longer simply watching troubleshooting. They were being taught how to discover documentation, reason through evidence, explain their conclusions, and eventually operate independently.

This week also began shaping a larger training concept:

Assess → Normalize → Train → Validate

Read Week 12 — From Technician to Technical Leader →

Week 13 — Measurable Team Impact

By Week 13, the journal had become more than a record of individual ticket work.

It had become evidence of progression: from learning the environment, to independent diagnostics, to preventing incorrect repair paths, building evidence-based escalations, mentoring technicians, and developing a repeatable training philosophy.

The question changed.

Not simply: What have I learned?

But instead:

What measurable effect is that learning beginning to have on the people and systems around me?

Theme: Turning technical growth into measurable evidence of readiness for greater responsibility.

Read Week 13 — Measurable Team Impact →

Week 14 — From Technician to Instructor

Week 14 began with the expectation of continued technical work, development of the triage-script concept, and informal mentoring.

Instead, I found myself conducting formal classroom training, coaching technicians on the data-center floor, adapting instruction to different skill paths, remediating knowledge gaps, and beginning to train leads.

“You're not diagnosing a ticket. You're diagnosing a problem.”

Ten technicians entered training at different points in their development.

  • 7 of 10 progressed to independently closing multiple tickets.
  • 2 technicians requiring additional development successfully progressed and closed tickets the same day.
  • 3 leads began participating in the training process.

The week also became the strongest real-world validation yet of:

Assess → Normalize → Train → Validate

The measure of progress had changed again.

I was no longer evaluating success only by the problems I could solve myself.

I was beginning to measure success by whether I could teach someone else to reason through those problems without me.

Theme: The transition from solving technical problems personally to developing technicians who can solve them independently.

The Six-Month Checkpoint

This journal began as a way to document whether I could successfully grow into an unfamiliar large-scale data-center environment.

But there was always another question underneath it.

Could that development eventually lead to greater responsibility, formal advancement, or a more substantial contribution to the organization?

The first fourteen weeks document the preparation.

What happened afterward was still unknown when this page was written.

Did I get the job?

Did the opportunity become something larger?

Did the training work develop into something permanent?

Did another opportunity change the direction entirely?


Was all of this worth it?
